# The Retraction Room: instructor guide

## The idea in one paragraph

Students are the emergency review board of the *Journal of Applied Business Behaviour*. In each of four 90-minute sessions they are locked in a room built around real studies, and they get out by reading tables, charts, field notes and transcripts correctly. Every room ends with a ruling: keep, correct or retract. Students calculate nothing; the skill being trained is reading and interpreting research.

| Room | Methods | Cases |
|---|---|---|
| 1 The Data Vault | sampling, descriptive statistics, histograms | Literary Digest poll (1936); Shu et al. honesty study (2012, retracted 2021) |
| 2 The Lab | experiments, ANOVA, p-values, regression, correlation vs causation | Power posing (2010) and its replication (2015); the beauty premium (1994) |
| 3 The Factory Floor | observation, field notes and coding, interviews, qualitative quality | The Hawthorne studies (1924–1932) and Levitt & List's re-analysis (2011) |
| 4 The Boardroom | focus groups, validity, field experiments, meta-analysis | New Coke (1985); the jam study (2000) and the choice-overload meta-analysis (2010) |

## Before the semester

1. Open the game, choose **Open the console** and enter your PIN.
2. Create one cohort per group, for example "Group A", "Group B" and "Group C". Each cohort gets a five-letter code. Students use it to join, and everything for that group is saved separately.
3. Tell students to read the study guide for Room 1 before the first session (about 25 minutes). The guides are open to everyone from the start page, with no code needed.

## A 90-minute session

| Time | What happens |
|---|---|
| 0–10 min | Open the room for this cohort in the console (Rooms → "Closed · click to open"). Students form teams of about 5 and join with the cohort code and a team name. One laptop per team is enough, and several laptops can join the same team and stay in sync. |
| 10–65 min | Teams play 5 locks. Each lock is worth 100 points, minus 5 per wrong code, and hints cost 5 and 15 points. A lock never drops below 40. Each lock links to the matching study-guide section. Watch progress in the console. If a team is stuck, you can open their current lock for them (they get 40 points). |
| 65–80 min | Debrief. The console shows two key points per lock. A good opener: "What did you almost get wrong?" The projector leaderboard is a good backdrop. |
| 80–90 min | Exit test: every student on their own, 8 questions, under their own name. It is not graded. Results appear in the console immediately. |

Teams keep the same name all semester and their scores add up to a series score. Leave a room open after the session if teams should be able to finish it later, or close it to pause play. Progress is saved either way.

## After each session

- **Download backup (.json)** for the cohort. It is a full snapshot of that cohort that you can load again later with **Load backup…**, either replacing the cohort or as a new copy.
- Glance at the exit test question analysis: a question that most students missed is a good start for the next lecture.

## End of semester and accreditation

- **Download results (Excel)** for each cohort, or **Download all results** for everything. The workbook has these sheets: About (game version and scoring rules), Cohorts, Team results, Exit tests (one row per student and room, with each question marked 1 or 0), Question analysis, and Answer key.
- **Archive** each cohort. That closes its rooms and stops new teams from joining, but keeps all data viewable and exportable.

## If something goes wrong

- *A team joined with a typo:* they can simply join again with the right name. The old entry stays visible in the console but does no harm.
- *A team's progress needs to restart:* in the console, choose **Reset room** next to the team. This affects only that room.
- *No internet or Firebase problems:* add `?local=1` to the address and the game runs on each laptop on its own. In that mode the console only sees the teams on the same laptop, so collect exit tests on paper.
